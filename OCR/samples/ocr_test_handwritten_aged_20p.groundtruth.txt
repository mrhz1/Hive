ADMISSION NOTE
17 Jan 1971    Ward C
This 51 year old married white male loom fixer is admitted
at 11.40 a.m. complaining of black tarry stools for 4 days
and increasing weakness. Referred from the office of Dr. J.
P. Mulcahy who found the haemoglobin 7.2 gm.
Patient states he has had burning epigastric pain for about
3 years, relieved by milk and by soda. Pain wakes him at 2
a.m. most nights. He has been taking soda powders from the
chemist and, latterly, aspirin for a bad shoulder - as many
as 12 tablets a day.
Four days ago he passed a large black stool, and again the
following morning. He felt faint at the loom on Friday and
was sent home by the foreman. No frank haematemesis. No
vomiting. Appetite poor for a fortnight. Weight down about
10 lb.
Past history: Pneumonia 1943 while in the Army. Appendix
out 1951, Union Hospital. Smokes 1 1/2 packs Camels daily
since age 17. Beer on Saturdays. No known drug allergy -
penicillin given 1943 without trouble.
Family: Father died 62, 'stomach trouble'. Mother living, 78,
diabetic. One brother, well. Two children, both well.
R. W. Ashcroft, M.D.

Consultation
25 Jan 1971    Requested by Dr. R. W. Ashcroft
Re:  Brennan, Arthur L.    Unit No. 22-84-016
Thank you for asking me to see this pleasant 51 year old mill
worker with a chronic duodenal ulcer which bled on 13-16 Jan.
I have reviewed the films with Dr. Okamura. The cap is
grossly deformed and there is a constant niche. The changes
are those of long-standing disease. There is no evidence of
pyloric obstruction: the stomach emptied well at 6 hours.
The haemorrhage stopped without operation and he has had
no further bleeding for 9 days. In my opinion there is no
present indication for surgery. Operation would be indicated
for recurrent severe haemorrhage, perforation, obstruction,
or intractable pain.
Recommendations:
  1.  Bland diet, six small feedings.
  2.  Antacid 1 and 3 hours after meals and h.s.
  3.  Absolute prohibition of aspirin and of the
      proprietary powders he has been using.
  4.  Stop smoking, or reduce to under half a pack.
  5.  Continue iron for 6 months.
  6.  Repeat upper G.I. in 3 months.
  7.  Office follow-up with Dr. Mulcahy in 2 weeks.
I shall be glad to see him again if any question arises.
G. Pemberton, M.D.

DISCHARGE SUMMARY
2 Feb 1971
Brennan, Arthur Leonard      Unit No. 22-84-016
Admitted 17 Jan 1971.   Discharged 2 Feb 1971.   16 days.
FINAL DIAGNOSES:
  1.  Chronic duodenal ulcer with haemorrhage.
  2.  Iron deficiency anaemia, secondary.
  3.  Chronic bronchitis and emphysema.
  4.  Salicylate ingestion, excessive.
This 51 year old loom fixer entered with a 4 day history of
melaena and a haemoglobin of 7.2 gm. He was treated with
bed rest, gastric lavage, hourly antacid, and 2 units of
whole blood on the day of admission. Bleeding ceased within
24 hours and did not recur.
Upper G.I. series on 20 Jan showed a deformed duodenal cap
with an active niche. Dr. Pemberton saw him in
consultation on 25 Jan and advised against operation.
Stools became guaiac negative on 24 Jan and remained so.
The haemoglobin rose steadily on oral iron to 12.4 gm at
discharge with a good reticulocyte response. He gained 5 lb.
and was ambulant and free of pain for the last ten days of
his stay.
The importance of avoiding aspirin was impressed upon him
repeatedly, this being in all likelihood the cause of the
haemorrhage.
R. W. Ashcroft, M.D.
